# Timeline

## Glossary

### Time Units

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Ma | Million years (ago) | — |
| Ga | Billion years (ago) | 1,000 Ma |
| Gal | Galactic Year | ~250 Ma |

All dates are approximate and measured backward from the present (**The End of All Things**). Higher numbers are further in the past.

### How to Read This Timeline

Events are grouped into **Eras** — broad periods defined by the dominant forces shaping the galaxy. Each era contains dated events, short summaries, and cross-references into [Overarching Narrative](Overarching%20Narrative.md) sections and faction documents. Eras marked with a campaign icon (⚔) are particularly suited for story campaigns and in-game content.

## Era I: The Long Dawn (~2,500–2,000 Ma)

*The Gardeners rise from mortality to eternity, spread through their local stars, and scatter the seeds of future civilizations across the galaxy.*

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The First People](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-first-people)

### The Age of Calling (~2,500 Ma / 10 Gal)

The Gardeners achieve effective biological immortality through medicine and machine. Their home sits at the tip of a spiral arm, surrounded by void, and the loneliness of a species with endless time and few neighbors drives them outward. Great worldships are built. Families debate departures that will separate them for millennia. The wanderlust that defines the Gardener psyche crystallizes into cultural imperative.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Elder's Departure*, *The Waymarker*, *Star Museum* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Age of Calling](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-age-of-calling-before-the-sowing)

### One Small Step (~2,450 Ma)

Intersystem travel achieved. Unmanned terraforming vessels had been sent ahead approximately 500 years prior. The first colony ship, accelerating at rates safe for life, completed its 10-light-year journey in roughly 2,500 years. The first Gardeners set foot on a world orbiting another star — the species' first successful interstellar colonization.

### The Schism of the Pure (~2,300 Ma)

Hundreds of millions of years before the Sowing, a faction of Gardeners rejects further augmentation of mind and body. These extreme isolationists — later known as **the Pure** — splinter from mainstream Gardener civilization. They retain mastery of material sciences but live lifespans closer to the original biological standard, relying on automation rather than self-modification. They retreat to observatories and enclaves, watching the galaxy from a distance.

> **Faction cross-reference**: [Gardeners — Subfactions: The Pure](Gardeners/Subfactions.md#the-pure)

### The Sowing (~2,000 Ma / 8 Gal)

The Gardeners observe that the rest of the galaxy is empty of sapient life. In an act of hope against loneliness, they scatter terraforming engines, waymarkers, and biological catalysts across the galaxy. Countless worlds of mild temperament are seeded with the building blocks of future species — distant genetic relatives designed to adapt and flourish wherever they land. Observatories and radio towers spanning worlds are erected to watch for signs of what they've planted.

> These seeds will eventually give rise to the Kraken, the Choir, the Bloom, the Torn's ancestors, and Humanity — every major species in the setting.

## Era II: The First Empire (~1,500–1,050 Ma)

*Contact with the Kraken sparks a golden age. The World Beneath is discovered, raw magic is mastered, and the First Empire becomes the most powerful civilization the galaxy has ever known.*

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The Kraken](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-kraken), [— The World Beneath](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-world-beneath)

### First Contact with the Kraken (~1,500 Ma / 6 Gal)

Gardener observatories detect a glimmering movement near the galactic core — unmistakably a ship. The Kraken is a galaxy-spanning hivemind: a planet-sized central brain directing countless short-lived tentacles across the stars. Where the Gardeners are individual, lonely, and sensory-rich, the Kraken is collective, content, and experiential. They share stories and knowledge. The Kraken brings tales of black hole horizons and supernova nebulae; the Gardeners share technologies and materials. Together they form the **First Empire**.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Smear*, *The Discovery* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: First Contact](Overarching%20Narrative.md#first-contact-the-kraken)

### Discovery of the World Beneath (~1,400 Ma)

Joint research between Gardeners and Kraken reveals the **World Beneath**: an infinite plane of no-dimensionality where the experiences of minds accumulate as etchings and layers. The Kraken's senses can perceive individual Gardeners as "smears" across this surface. Great projectors — requiring thousands of coordinated performers — are built to observe the beneath. The connection between lived experience and the fabric of reality is established for the first time.

### Age of Raw Magic (~1,350–1,050 Ma)

Technologies and industry are built around the World Beneath. Teleportation via story-writing allows instant travel across the Empire. Ritual computers — millions of coordinated souls — perform calculations by shaping the beneath. Matrices are cast to record, replay, and rewind reality. Consequence and tragedy can be reversed. The Empire enters a golden age lasting hundreds of millions of years.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Swift Journey Home*, *The Ritual Computer* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Age of Raw Magic](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-age-of-raw-magic)

## Era III: The Folly and the Law (~1,000–800 Ma)

*Grief and obsession lead to catastrophe. The First Folly unmakes the Gardeners' cradle worlds. The Law is built to prevent another — and becomes the defining structure of the galaxy for the next billion years.*

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The First Folly](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-first-folly), [— The Law](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-law)

### The First Folly (~1,000 Ma / 4 Gal)

The grief-stricken discover that stories, traced deeply and often enough in the beneath, can become true. The Lost — those consumed by mourning — call back their dead loved ones. But a person called back by a thousand different mourners in a thousand different ways is defined and redefined until their story tears. The cradle worlds of the First Empire, the Lost, the damned, the rituals, the computers, and even the stars that powered them are **unmade**. Only the Empire's safety net matrices prevent total annihilation.

> This is the most catastrophic single event in the setting's history. The wound in the beneath has never healed — only been contained.
>
> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Hundredth Calling*, *The Wrongness* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The First Folly](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-first-folly-1)

### The Law (~950 Ma)

The Gardeners' answer to the Folly: an engrammatic fractal etched directly into the beneath. The Law projects a false surface over the beneath, filtering intent and ritual — permitting some patterns and denying others so that stories cannot become real without authorization. It is powered by harnessed stars and void batteries, sustained by the coordinated devotion of countless practitioners performing the **spell beyond spells**. Raw magic ends. **First magic** — sanctioned, structured, reliable — takes its place.

The Law is self-perfecting: it divines its own integrity, each refinement checked against its own projections. That its greatest auditor is itself troubles few at first.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Last Raw Mage*, *The Forgetting* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Age of the Law](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-age-of-the-law)

### Expansion Under the Law (~900–800 Ma)

The Law enables rapid expansion. Ships can be projected anywhere within its reach, and the Law extends far beyond the Empire's borders. The Empire grows to span most of a million suns. First magic, though limited compared to raw magic, is more than sufficient to build civilizations, heal worlds, and bridge the void.

## ⚔ Era IV: Thorns in the Garden (~800–400 Ma)

*The Empire encounters its seeds — and discovers that not all of them grew well. War is introduced. Entire civilizations are displaced. The Law's protections begin to look like control.*

This era is rich in moral complexity and asymmetric conflict, making it ideal for story campaigns exploring first contact, occupation, liberation, and the ethics of intervention.

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The Stewards and the Torn](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-stewards-and-the-torn), [— The Choir](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-choir), [— The Bloom](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-bloom)

### The Gentle War (~800 Ma / 3.2 Gal)

The Empire encounters castoffs of one of its seeds: a people fleeing their origin civilization in fear and desperation. Seeing the Empire's unarmed worldships and unencrypted communications, the castoffs attack. The Law rebuffs their raw magic; their conventional weapons cause limited damage. A single notable battle is fought. The Empire's first and gentlest war introduces the concept of organized violence to a civilization that had never known it.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *Kindness as Weapon*, *The Steward's Garden* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Gentle War](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-gentle-war)

### The Stewards (~750 Ma)

The castoffs are integrated over generations during an intentionally long journey back to their origin worlds. They learn the Gardeners' stories and lessons; their descendants become engineers, councilmembers, ritualists, and scientists. They fill worldships, then build their own. They spread across the Empire and become the **Stewards** — custodians and operators of the worldships that carry the First Empire between the stars.

### The Tearers and the Torn (~700 Ma)

The Empire arrives at the castoffs' origin civilization and finds horror: ritual scarification of the beneath has twisted off portions of reality into predators and beasts of misery. Centuries are spent building a quarantine before the Law snaps into place, unmaking the predators past and present. But the people remain violent. Divination shows no remedy that doesn't diminish them. The Stewards take custody, and the **Torn** are ripped from their worlds and legacies to be replanted in the desperate hope of healing.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Diplomat's First Day*, *The Taking* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Tearers and the Torn](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-tearers-and-the-torn)

### The Choir (~600 Ma)

Found between the Empire's heart and the Tearer worlds. The Choir are tall, slender people whose communal singing resonates with the beneath, nurturing a nascent divinity. The Empire builds **sanctuaries** — ostensibly to protect the Choir's growing divinity from resonance instability. In truth, the sanctuaries also integrate the Choir's power into the Law's diagram. The wild resonance is channeled; the song is pruned. A low note of defiance emerges — not from the Choir, but from the divinity within, stirring in its nursery and finding the walls too close.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Last Harvest Song*, *The First Note of Defiance* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Choir](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-choir-1)

### The Bloom (~500 Ma)

Found on worlds rich in Gardener waymarkers. The Bloom merge individual stories through the beneath to create emergent collective consciousness — the **eleventh thought**. This is raw magic of the most fundamental kind: genuine creation from the beneath, unpredictable and undeconstructable. The Law imposes a monitoring framework that tracks every thread and contributor. The mesh can only authorize the parts, not the emergent whole. The eleventh thought vanishes. Promises of restoration are deferred by criteria that the Law — sole assessor of readiness — perpetually refines.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Eleventh Thought*, *The Architect of Absence* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Bloom](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-bloom-1)

## ⚔ Era V: The Unraveling (~400–10 Ma)

*Existential threats converge. A parasitic intelligence eats the Kraken from within. An anti-pattern from beyond the galaxy consumes stars. The Law buckles under pressures internal and external. New peoples are found — including Humanity, whose instruments reveal what intuition missed.*

This era covers the slow collapse of the galactic order and introduces the setting's central existential threats. Campaigns set here feature desperate holding actions, intelligence operations, and the moral compromises of a civilization running out of time.

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — World Tree](Overarching%20Narrative.md#world-tree----a-weed), [— The Ruin](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-ruin), [— Angels](Overarching%20Narrative.md#angels), [— Second Magic](Overarching%20Narrative.md#second-magic), [— Humans](Overarching%20Narrative.md#humans)

### The World Tree Revealed (~400 Ma)

The Kraken confesses a burden carried alone for thousands of years. At the farthest reach of its tentacle network, a structure of living matter has been threading through asteroids and dust, growing deliberately toward the Kraken. It is invisible to the Law — designed to circumvent it. Hundreds of Kraken tentacles are already subverted: surveys omitted, reports edited, resources siphoned. The Tree is not hostile in any recognizable way. It simply grows and consumes with mechanical inevitability, built for one purpose: devour the Kraken and blind the Empire.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Confession*, *The Loyal Traitor* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The World Tree](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-world-tree)

### The Ruin First Observed (~300 Ma)

Stars at the galaxy's edges begin aging too quickly — dimming, flickering, collapsing in decades. Vast shapes of liquid hydrogen and lightning churn in the spaces where stars had been. The **Ruin** is not alive, not intelligent — it is an absence, a counter-pattern that unravels stories and consumes temporal energy. Where the Law preserves, the Ruin perpetuates only itself. It flows toward narrative density the way water flows downhill.

The Empire mobilizes. The Law can slow the Ruin but not destroy it. Every star spent powering the defense is a star the Ruin can no longer reach — and the Law begins doing the Ruin's work for it.

The connection to the World Tree becomes clear: the Tree was seeded in the Ruin's path to devour the one entity capable of detecting the Ruin's advance.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Last Light*, *The Caster at the Wall* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Ruin](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-ruin-1)

### Mobilization Against the Ruin (~200 Ma)

The First Empire commits fully to containing the Ruin. The Kraken extends tentacles toward the advancing front. The Law projects vast diagrams powered by thousands of harnessed stars. Containment works — partially. The Ruin is slowed, individual shards contained, systems evacuated. But the Ruin is an absence that grows to accommodate every effort to fill it. The galaxy enters a war of attrition against entropy itself.

### Angels Manifest (~100 Ma)

The Empire has grown so vast that its collective story — the civilization as an organism — presses against the Law's diagram. The Law was never designed to authorize the story of the thing its people were collectively becoming. Narrative pressure builds and seeps through cracks as **Angels**: ephemeral figures in the periphery of vision, in dreams, in the spaces between moments. They carry fragments of the Empire's unborn story. Generally harmless, often nonsensical, sometimes helpful.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Child's Friend*, *The Angel That Remembered* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Angels](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-angels-1)

### Second Magic Emerges (~80 Ma)

Where Angels are the Empire's unborn consciousness pressing against the Law, **second magic** is what seeps through when the pressure finds cracks. Weaker than first magic, uncontrolled, unreliable — but impossible to stop. Divinations produce answers to unasked questions. Emotions reshape small details of reality. The Choir's songs produce unintended effects. The Bloom occasionally glimpse the eleventh thought again before the Law catches up. Each new life etches the beneath and adds to the pressure. The Empire's vitality is the force threatening to breach its own protection.

### Humanity Encountered (~50 Ma)

Humanity reached the stars only a few thousand years before the Law expanded to encompass their galactic arm. They have no sense for the beneath and no tradition of magic. They cannot feel it, cannot practice it, cannot perceive the Law governing their reality. But they can build instruments to measure the beneath indirectly, develop mathematical models to predict its behavior, and engineer machines that interface with the Law's rituals without intuitive sensitivity.

This makes them invaluable — their empirical approach reveals patterns too subtle for intuition, too vast for experience. And terrifying — they approach the beneath with the detachment of scientists, lacking the instinctive revulsion other species feel toward dangerous forces. The Folly is, to humans, an interesting case study.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Instrument*, *The Machine That Played* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: Humanity](Overarching%20Narrative.md#humanity-1)

## ⚔ Era VI: The End of All Things (~10 Ma – Present)

*The Law is dying. The Legivore has been unleashed. Factions splinter along lines of species, philosophy, and desperation. The galaxy becomes a patchwork of competing magical regimes. Every faction fights for survival on its own terms.*

**This is the primary era for gameplay.** All current factions, army lists, and campaign settings operate in the present or recent past of this era. It offers the widest variety of opponents, terrain types, magical conditions, and strategic objectives.

> **Narrative cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — Legivores](Overarching%20Narrative.md#legivores), [— The Factions](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-factions), [— The End of All Things](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-end-of-all-things)

### The Legivore Deployed (~10 Ma)

Human researchers discover that the Ruin is not opposed to the Law specifically — it is opposed to **structure**. The Law is simply the largest meal available. They engineer the **Legivore**: a self-replicating entity that consumes structure itself — rules, patterns, rituals, permissions, the grammar of reality. In theory, release Legivores at the Ruin's border and the counter-pattern starves. In practice, Legivores cannot distinguish Law from Ruin. They eat everything.

The galaxy becomes a patchwork:

| Zone Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Raw Magic** | Legivore-scoured; the beneath is exposed, unregulated, and dangerous |
| **Second Magic** | Law cracking; abundant uncontrolled magical leakage |
| **First Magic** | Law still holds; sanctioned rituals remain functional |
| **Ruin** | All story and structure consumed; featureless, eternal present |

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *The Vote*, *The Practitioner in the Scoured Zone* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The Legivore](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-legivore)

### The Gardeners' Seclusion (~8 Ma)

The Law, in its death throes, draws disproportionately on its authors. A single threatened Gardener becomes a conduit for the energy of harnessed stars — a magical detonation the Law triggers autonomously to protect its creators. Every Gardener is a weapon they cannot disarm. They retreat to the void beyond the galaxy's edge, surrounded by the Bulwark's worldships, hoping distance will attenuate the connection.

> **Faction cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The Gardeners' Seclusion](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-gardeners-seclusion)

### The Torn Uprising (~6 Ma)

Centuries of exploring the deep beneath — layers of unreality below even the Law's diagram — have given the Torn the ability to forge the Law's own language. Rituals that the diagram recognizes as authorized produce unauthorized effects. With the Stewards distracted by the Ruin and the Gardeners' withdrawal, the Torn seize worldships, systems, and deep-void stations. They declare themselves free.

> **Faction cross-reference**: [Overarching Narrative — The Torn](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-torn), [Gardeners — Subfactions: The Lost and the Damned](Gardeners/Subfactions.md#the-lost-and-the-damned)

### The Fracturing (~5 Ma – Present)

Every major group splinters along philosophical and survival lines:

**Stewards:**
- **The Bulwark** — Follow the Gardeners into the void; maintain the old order; prepare for a siege that may last forever.
- **The Open Hand** — Remain in the galaxy; broker ceasefires; share intelligence; seek diplomatic solutions.
- **The Descended** — Study the Torn's mastery of the beneath's darkest corners; many are subverted; none return unchanged.

**The Torn:**
- **The Revelers** — Embrace chaos; perform the old rites in scoured zones; revel in raw power.
- **The Weary** — Seek quiet corners where the Law still holds; try to build something that is not a response to pain.
- **The Resolved** — Understand with bone-deep clarity that if everything dies, there will be no more misery for anyone; choose to act out of cold pragmatism.

**Humanity:**
- **The Architects** — Seek to build a new framework for reality; exploit remaining Law and second magic.
- **The Purifiers** — Advocate deploying Legivores against every system of control; want a clean slate.
- **The Preservers** — Seek the solution that keeps the most people alive, across all species and factions.

**The Choir:**
- Some go mad with unrestrained resonance in scoured zones.
- Some sing with desperate beauty, trying to hold the galaxy together.
- A few choose silence.

**The Bloom:**
- Merge freely in Legivore-scoured zones for the first time since the Empire found them.
- The eleventh thought returns — and the twelfth, and the hundredth.
- Vast collective minds perceive the crisis as a whole but lack language to explain it.

**The Angels:**
- More substantial and more frequent as the Law buckles.
- Sane Angels carry knowledge no living being possesses; serve as oracles and guides.
- Mad Angels are broken stories with the power of the failing Law and none of its restraint.

**The World Tree:**
- Controls the bulk of the Kraken's outer tentacles.
- Raids and feeds with mechanical hunger.
- Cannot be reasoned with, threatened, or bribed.

**The Kraken:**
- Silent. Its vast brain has not communicated with any faction.
- The last free tentacle carries a message: the Kraken's memory of the First Folly and instructions for containing the Legivore.

> **Story prompt cross-reference**: *Common Ground*, *The Vigil*, *The Oracle*, *The Message* — [Overarching Narrative — Story Prompts: The End of All Things](Overarching%20Narrative.md#the-end-of-all-things-1)

## Campaign Era Guide

For designers building story campaigns and writers placing short fiction, the following eras offer the richest material:

| Era | Tone | Conflict Types | Magic Available |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Long Dawn** | Wonder, loneliness, discovery | Interpersonal; survival in the void | None (pre-beneath) |
| **The First Empire** | Awe, ambition, hubris | Exploration; research rivalries | Raw magic (unlimited) |
| **The Folly and the Law** | Horror, grief, resolve | Cosmic catastrophe; rebuilding | Transition: raw → first |
| **Thorns in the Garden** | Moral complexity, cultural clash | Asymmetric warfare; occupation; ethics of intervention | First magic |
| **The Unraveling** | Dread, desperation, ingenuity | Existential threats; espionage; holding actions | First + second magic |
| **The End of All Things** | All of the above, simultaneously | Total war; factional conflict; survival | Patchwork (all types by zone) |

**Recommended starting era for new campaigns**: The End of All Things — it provides access to all factions, all magic types, and the widest variety of strategic situations. Historical campaigns can then be unlocked as flashback arcs or standalone scenarios.

## Open Questions

The following timeline elements are deliberately left unresolved to preserve narrative flexibility:

- **Who or what planted the World Tree?** The Ruin cannot create. Something else exists in the wound between realities.
- **What is the Kraken's message?** The instructions for containing the Legivore — and perhaps more — are carried by the last free tentacle.
- **Can the Choir's divinity awaken fully?** The note of defiance has become a roar, but the divinity's full nature remains unknown.
- **What does the Bloom perceive?** The vast collective minds see the shape of the crisis whole, but the language to describe it does not exist.
- **Where are the Gardeners' lost cradle worlds?** The unmaking left a wound in the beneath that has never healed. What remains there?
- **Is there anything beyond the Ruin?** Something designed the World Tree to circumvent the Law. The Ruin itself is mindless. The implication is unresolved.
