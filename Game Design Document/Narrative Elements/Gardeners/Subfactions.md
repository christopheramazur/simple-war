# The Pure
## Summary Blurb
Extreme isolationists who eschew alterations to their minds and bodies. 

## General Overview
Splintered from the Gardeners hundreds of millions of years before the Seeding, the Pure know nothing about the beneath, and only know about other species and the various ongoing conflicts in the galaxy through their observatories. 

They still have incredible mastery of material sciences, but their lifetimes are more in-line with human standard. Heavy use of automation machinery ensures they are able to live fulfilling lives in pursuit of the moment. 

# Lawmakers
## Summary Blurb
Diviners. Spellcrafters. Practitioners. The chosen few, called to serve. 

## General Overview
Empowered with the raw magic they help keep leashed, the stations of the Law are the most impactful an individual can aspire to -- and the most isolating.

Despite the overwhelming majority of Lawmakers being Gardeners who have touched the divine before and will again in the future, the training is mandatory with every enlistment. The physical, mental, and magical requirements are exacting, and even a short millennia away is enough to have lost touch with . The routine and schedule leave little room for individuality.



# Voidfarers
## Summary Blurb
Wanderers. Wayfarers. The ones who never stopped reaching for the next horizon.

## General Overview
The Voidfarers are those Gardeners for whom the void between stars is not a thing to endure but a thing to embrace. Where most Gardeners felt the loneliness of the empty dark and built worldships to carry their homes with them, the Voidfarers found something else in the silence: clarity, and purpose, and a freedom from the noise of civilizations that grew too loud for their senses.

They were the first to leave the cradle worlds voluntarily, the ones who built the waymarkers and scattered the seeds, who crewed the research vessels and pushed the boundaries of the Empire's reach further than anyone thought wise. Their augmentations are tuned for the void — hardened against radiation, efficient in their energy needs, their senses adapted to read the faint signals of deep space rather than the overwhelming stimuli of habitable worlds.

In the age of the First Empire, the Voidfarers were the connective tissue between far-flung worlds. They crewed the great worldships that carried the Stewards between the stars. They made first contact with more of the seeded civilizations than any other Gardener tradition. They found the Choir, the Bloom, and countless lesser peoples on worlds the rest of the Empire had forgotten about.

When the Gardeners retreated to the void, the Voidfarers were already there. Some joined the seclusion willingly, guiding their kin to the deep places they had mapped over millions of years. Others refused to stop — scattered across the galaxy in small fleets and lone vessels, running dark to avoid the Law's attention, carrying messages and stories and technologies between the fragments of a dying civilization. They trade with anyone. They fight for no one by default. Their ships appear at the edges of battles and blockades and burning systems, offering aid or passage or simply bearing witness, and then they are gone.

Where the Cradle keeps the Gardeners together and the Lawmakers keep the Law intact, the Voidfarers keep the Gardeners connected to the galaxy they seeded. They are the eyes and ears of a species in hiding, the ones who know which routes are still safe and which stars have gone dark and where the Legivore-scoured zones offer passage free from the Law's dying grip. Many carry augmentations so extensive they are barely recognizable as the sensory-rich beings their ancestors were — hulls of machinery wrapped around a Gardener core, optimized for decades or centuries alone in the dark between systems.

The Voidfarers are the loneliest of an already lonely people, and they would not have it any other way.

# Cradle
## Summary Blurb
Home is not a place. It is a promise to return.

## General Overview
The Cradle are the heart of Gardener civilization — the keepers of the hearth, the builders of homes, the ones who remember why the worldships were built in the first place. Where the Voidfarers reached for the next horizon, the Cradle reached for the people beside them, and never let go.

The original cradle worlds are gone — unmade in the First Folly, along with the Lost and the damned and the stars that powered them. But the Cradle is not a place. It is a tradition older than the Empire, rooted in the Gardeners' nature as a filial people with deep bonds and longer memories. The Cradle built the schedules that kept families close across light-years. They maintained the communities that gave returning wanderers a reason to come home. They were the ones whose farewell celebrations were the most extravagant, because they felt the departures most keenly.

After the Folly, the Cradle took on a different weight. They became the stewards of grief — the ones who tended the wound of the Lost not by forgetting, as the broader culture chose to do, but by remembering what was loved without reaching for what was gone. They shaped the rituals of mourning that allowed the Gardeners to live alongside their greatest tragedy without being consumed by it again.

When the Law began its death spiral and the Gardeners retreated to the void, the Cradle organized the seclusion. They built the communities in exile, established the routines and traditions that keep an immortal people sane in the featureless dark beyond the galaxy's edge. They maintain the genealogies, the story-archives, the song-cycles that preserve the Gardeners' identity across geological time. The Bulwark worldships protect the perimeter; the Cradle tends everything inside it.

The Cradle's relationship to the Law is intimate and fraught. They are the tradition most deeply intertwined with the spell beyond spells — the first heartbeat that sustained the Law was born from the devotion of Gardeners to their communities, and the Cradle's communal rituals remain among the most potent remaining conduits for the Law's power. This makes their enclaves in the void disproportionately dangerous; the Law draws on their stories more heavily than any other Gardener tradition, and the protective detonations it triggers on their behalf are correspondingly devastating.

In the rare instances where a Cradle force takes the field, they fight as they live — together, close, and with a cohesion born of millions of years of community. They are slow to anger and slower to forget, and they will endure anything if it means their people can continue.

# The Lost and the Damned
## Summary Blurb
Walking weapons. Hidden, hunted, and carrying the weight of a billion years of good intentions.

## General Overview
The Lost and the Damned are the Gardeners who stayed behind.

When the majority of their kind retreated to the void beyond the galaxy's edge, fleeing the Law's lethal devotion to its creators, not all of them went. Some were too deeply embedded in the galaxy to extract — researchers, diplomats, practitioners who had spent millennia building lives among the other peoples of the Empire. Some refused to leave, unwilling to abandon the species they had seeded and the responsibilities they had assumed. Some simply could not bear another departure; the Gardeners are a people prone to loneliness, and the void beyond the void was the loneliest place they could imagine.

They are the Lost because they are separated from their people — cut off from the seclusion, scattered across the galaxy in ones and twos and small hidden enclaves, their identities concealed, their stories muffled to avoid the Law's attention. They are the Damned because the Law will not let them go. Every Gardener is a conduit for the dying Law's protective fury; when one is threatened, the Law channels the energy of harnessed stars through them, a magical detonation that destroys the threat and everything around it. They did not ask for this. They cannot disarm it. Their very existence endangers everyone they are trying to protect.

The name echoes deliberately. The original Lost were those grief-stricken Gardeners whose obsessive mourning caused the First Folly — who called back the dead so many times and in so many ways that reality itself tore. The original damned were those unmade alongside them. The modern Lost and the Damned carry that legacy not as perpetrators but as inheritors: the knowledge that the Gardeners' greatest catastrophes have always been born from love that could not let go.

Some hide among the Stewards' worldships, their augmentations disguised, their stories rewritten to read as something other than Gardener. Some have found refuge with the Torn — a bitter irony, given that the Gardeners once displaced the Torn in the name of healing. Some wander alone, avoiding populated systems entirely, knowing that a moment of danger could turn them into a weapon that levels a city. A few have sought out the Legivore-scoured zones, where the Law cannot reach and the raw beneath offers freedom from the curse — at the cost of exposure to everything the Law was built to prevent.

The Torn Uprising complicated things further. When the Torn seized worldships and deep-void stations, the Lost and the Damned hiding among the Stewards were caught between sides — too dangerous to either faction to be ignored, too valuable to either to be discarded. Some were exposed and detonated, the Law's protective fury incinerating the very people the Gardeners had once sworn to heal. Others were sheltered by sympathetic Torn who understood, better than most, what it meant to be defined by what had been done to you rather than what you chose.

On the battlefield, the Lost and the Damned are a paradox: individuals of extraordinary capability who dare not use it. Their augmentations are ancient and powerful. Their understanding of the beneath is deeper than almost any living being's. But every engagement carries the risk of triggering the Law's protective reflex, and the resulting detonation does not distinguish between friend and enemy. They fight with precision and restraint, trying to end conflicts before they escalate — because if they cannot, the escalation will be catastrophic.
