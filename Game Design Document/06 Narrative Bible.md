# 6. Narrative Bible

What is canon after the design interview. The pre-interview lore lives in `History/Narrative Elements/`: Overarching Narrative, Timeline, and the Gardeners documents (The First People, the Kraken, the World Beneath, the First Folly, the Stewards and the Torn, the Choir, the Bloom). It is **unreviewed source material**: where it conflicts with this page, this page wins, and the timeline and narrative interviews will reconcile it (R8Q16).

## 6.1 Scope and theme
- **Era:** the story is set in **The End of All Things (EoAT)**. The tutorial sits in a "first contact" sub-era just before it. Earlier eras appear only as exposition (R1Q13).
- **Shape:** the story is time-linear, like StarCraft / Brood War, and starts with a human character (R1Q12). A story state machine flexible enough for faction-order-independent play is a stretch goal.
- **Not concluded:** Simple War doesn't conclude the story; the scope is Halo-like (R3Q15). Humanity is a latecomer and the **catalyst** for the Legivore. It destroys magic at its root, which ripples outward and calls back predators long gone from this corner of reality.
- **Core theme** (R3Q15): how do individuals and organisations reconcile an all-out fight for existence with staying human, when defeat could leave nothing to know what "human" was? The message has to go deeper than shallow themes (R4Q23).
- **Reality is fraying** (R1Q14): the war is existential, and humanity survives by overcoming impossible odds again and again. Re-enacting legends makes legendary results possible, and they perpetuate themselves.

## 6.2 Magic (R1Q15, R2Q8, R3Q19, R4Q21)
| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Raw magic** | The primal narrative force. It can do anything, but needs a massive ritual framework to direct it |
| **First Magic: the Law** | An operating system built on raw magic, with a registry and audits. It governs access to magic |
| **Second Magic** | Illicit or fragmented access to Law endpoints, through narrative backpressure, ritual shims and exploits. It is *not* degraded capability |
| **Ruin** | Law scoured beyond function. Costs rise, efficacy falls and types are restricted, whatever the access path (like denial-of-service or 500 errors) |
| **Null** | Magic scoured away entirely |

Where the Law fails, magic fails. Raw magic does not become available, barring "wacky" circumstances.

## 6.3 Cosmology
- **Before the Gardeners** (R4Q21, R5Q14): the Gardeners were late bloomers, in a universe already picked over by early gods and their predators.
- **The World Tree:** an early god that stayed. It is crippled transcendence, self-sustaining, a local apotheosis, and it can be materially eradicated. Predators passed it over like a sea anemone, not worth picking apart. World Tree followers may become a future faction.
- **The returning predators:** bottom-feeders, transcendent beings who accept material existence in exchange for fewer threats and more abundant, low-quality sustenance. The rapidly unbinding Law calls them back, through echoes of its impending birth and death. Only the Law, the Law As It Could Have Been, or the Legivore can divert them.
- **Foreshadowing only** in Simple War (R5Q14).

## 6.4 The road to Angelfall (R2Q10, R4Q22)
1. **The Law near apotheosis.** The First Empire never imagined it could get so bad, and loses trust in itself.
2. **The Tearers.** Their horrors shook faith in divination.
3. **The Legivore cannot be divined** (it devoured divining magic). That divergence became the **First Angel**. The Law's pressure turned from a careful dam for apotheosis into an ocean of burning kerosene.
   - **Lawbringers followed impossible futures.** Avoidance paths lay against the Law.
   - **The Last Angel to come.** Everything up to the Law's demise is primed to become the **Last Angel**: the Law As It Could Have Been, perverted.
4. **First contact with humanity.** Lawbringer diplomats arrive, and the Law is extended over human space (R2Q10).
   - **What the Lawbringers showed humanity:**
     - its seeding
     - the failed waystones
     - the embryonic veil
   - **What they withheld:** magic and technology. They had divined war and billions lost.
   - **Their creed:** a culture of honesty, and "guide but not provide".
   - **Humanity's response:** entitlement erupted ("birthright").
   - **Venting:** the Law vented its pressure onto primitive human planets as harmless manifestations.
5. **The Legivore spreads.** Angels were thought to have whispered the first Legivore to madmen.
   - **Sol cut off:** the Legivore's first deployment severed Sol's Law from the Empire.
   - **The swarm:** self-replicating probes and trillions of Legivore ramjets followed. Most were intercepted, but countless worlds were Ruined.
6. **Imperial triage.**
   - **Zero trust:** the Empire severed divination streams, Ruined conveyance and went zero-trust. Manual rites consumed Gardener lives.
   - **The Law dragged backward** at the moment of apotheosis.
   - **The True Believers:** the Law showed visions of ruin and betrayal, which produced the first Lawbringer splinter, the **True Believers**.
7. **Silence before the fall.** Humanity saw none of this. Angels vanished because the Law could no longer vent, so pressure built unchecked, and humanity rejoiced.
8. **The first Angelfall:** the tutorial.

Timeline (R2Q9): humans are long-lived, functionally immortal on established planets. First contact to Angelfall is within 1,000 years, and the EoAT follows shortly after Angelfall (5–30 years). The old "~50 Ma" date is an error. The exact dates wait for the timeline interview.

## 6.5 The Legivore (R3Q19)
- **Name versus nature:** it is named for what people see (devouring Law), but it actually **kills magic**, like acetone on styrofoam.
- **Etching:** it can etch restrictions out of the Law locally. Its simplest use is to render down and burn.
- **Aftermath:** Ruined areas are the Law scoured beyond function; where magic is scoured entirely, the result is a Null zone.
- **Role:** the main cause of Angelfalls, and the reason the Law dies instead of reaching apotheosis.

## 6.6 Angels and Angelfall (R2Q10, R3Q17)
- **What Angels are:** lensed counterfactuals. Humanity's broken history, plus divination-circumvention tools (the proto-Legivore), plus the Law's narrative pressure, produce "angels". They are the things people wish they'd done or said: "messengers from nonsense".
- **Left alone:** unaddressed, Angels build a counternarrative, like a sand barrier against water.
- **Angelfall:** planetary failure, forcing evacuation. It spreads from where the Law is weakest and eventually takes whole systems. It builds over a long time and is locally unstoppable once triggered. Enough Lawbringers, backed by a node and unsabotaged, could stop one.
- **Rarity:** Angelfalls are rare. The player prevents one about **once** in the story, dramatically. Every Angel not fought returns in that campaign.
- **Not a player faction:** Angels are an undirected narrative faction (R4Q25, R4Q26).

## 6.7 Factions
### Humanity (playable; R5Q12, R4Q23)
One faction on a shared core roster, with three splinters as a doctrine layer.

- **Humanity First:** the Legivore faction.
  - **Who they are:** fringe dissidents, shadowy benefactors and terrorist splinter cells, holding a Legivore stockpile.
  - **Message:** independence and superiority propaganda against the "colonising" Empire.
  - **Ties:** officers dissatisfied with CHI are in contact with the True Believers and warmongers. Humanity First is the gateway for other factions onto non-Law worlds, and first into the fray.
  - **Units:** powerful, charismatic commanders; discounted infantry and gear; weak upgrades and heavy armour.
  - **Campaign feel:** where the player's hardship choices matter most: defying leadership, beating the odds.
- **CHI (Conglomerate for Human Integration):** a cooperative backed by the UTG, with Lawbringer ties.
  - **Military:** the most advanced military, with Lawbringer-trained operatives and limited First Magic.
  - **Role:** the humanitarian "good guys".
  - **Campaign feel:** bonus objectives pull the player's attention, and high-maintenance characters and bureaucrats fight over every penny. CHI is denied the superweapon. A traditional campaign.
- **The Enlightened:** they glimpsed the Law and think they can do better. They want a brilliant strategist to play warlord, end the war fast, unfreeze assets the UTG has frozen, and settle far from the Law to prove it.

Development order: the Humanity base for Duel first, with the three splinters as variants (R5Q16).

### Lawbringers (R3Q16, R4Q25)
- **Who they are:** primarily Gardeners, but any species can join. Their dedication is magically ascertained and reinforced, and First Magic compensates for physical or intellectual limits. The first human Lawbringers are a future story beat.
- **What they do:** they are the organisation sustaining the Law's rituals, with divisions for diplomacy, peacekeeping, humanitarian aid and warfare.
- **In play:** a faction separate from the Gardeners, with no subfactions. Each Lawbringer is either identical to the others or a named character with emergency powers.
  - **Campaigns:** raid-boss-tier commanders with few auxiliaries or constructs.
  - **PvP:** they rely on commander power, in the vein of Brood War Protoss: rigid, forcing the pace, punishing mistakes, snowballing.
- **Development order:** the second faction (R5Q16).

### Others
- **True Believers:** a campaign-only antagonist using Lawbringer assets (R5Q15).
- **Ruin, the Legivore and the World Tree:** non-playable threats (R1Q11).
- **Voidfarers:** neutral or event factions (R1Q11).

## 6.8 The player and Jeff
- **The player** creates a self-insert Commander. Campaigns may cast them as other commanders, or several (R1Q10).
- **Death:** story-essential commanders revive only on easy (R2Q13).
- **Relationships:** dispositions live in the sim; Dialogic presents them (R3Q18).
- **Jeff** (R1Q10, R2Q10, R3Q17): a conspiracy theorist on a backwater frontier planet. He spotted the Angelfall pattern through schizoid paranoia, which shows that absurd options can be right, though he is wrong about everything else.
  - **He recurs.** If he is mishandled, he retreats into seclusion and stops helping.
  - **Easter egg:** if the relationship is maintained, shortly before the preventable-Angelfall campaign Jeff says "angels almost never show up to a battle they can't lose". That line unlocks the inverted auto-resolve.

## 6.9 Tutorial mission: Angelfall (R1Q10, R7Q7, R5Q13, R6Q16)
- **Setting:** an early Angelfall on a frontier planet. Jeff is the last person outside the hangars, evacuating civilians for the **planetary AI**, which is locked out of the hangar controls.
- **Objective:** civilians are unarmed squads under the player's control, delivered to **hangar zones** that score Influence. Unlocking the hangars is a separate scripted objective, through the planetary AI's triggers.
- **Allies:** the planetary AI's robotic forces aid Jeff and guard civilians. They have **Capture Rate 0**, so civilians must be present for shuttles to take off.
- **Enemy:** Angels hunt the civilians. Movement-blocking Angels must be cleared by a commander with no battleforce (the inverted auto-resolve).
- **Development role:** Angelfall is the first vertical slice and the proving ground for the campaign tools.
- **In play (sw-136.2):** content lives in `data/packages/core/defs/angelfall.json`. Numbers are first-pass and wait for playtests.
  - **Roster:**
    - **Civilians:** unarmed, 6 HP, CR 10.
    - **Jeff:** his squad is Jeff with an old revolver plus two stragglers. The `jeff_commander` is tagged `story_essential`, and his army is civilians and a CR-0 patrol.
    - **CR-0 robots:** arc carbines, plating, CR 0, so they guard but can't board.
    - **Angels:** an Angel host (seraphs with short-range radiant lances) and an Angel choir (cantors with ranged choir light). Both deal magic damage, fly over terrain, and wear a veil that shrugs off mundane bullets and blasts.
  - **Overworld `angelfall_frontier`:**
    - **Towns:** three frontier towns produce civilians for free.
    - **The blockade:** cliffs wall off the spaceport, except a road hex held by a dispersible Angel blockade.
    - **The spaceport:** held by Angels that can't be dispersed. It's fought on `angelfall_spaceport`.
    - **Triggers:** Jeff's intro; dispersed Angels are counted in `angels_dispersed`; a second blockade cuts off the hill town on turn 4; winning the spaceport sets `evacuated`.
  - **Battle `angelfall_spaceport`:**
    - **The objective:** both hangar zones start locked. When player 1 takes the control console, the planetary AI opens them; it nudges the player on turn 2 if nobody has.
    - **Evacuating:** each turn civilians hold an open hangar, a shuttle lifts off (`shuttles`). The influence the hangars score wins the battle.
